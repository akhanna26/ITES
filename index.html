Methodology

The instrument

21 factors organized into 5 tiers. Gates are binary; the rest are scored 1–5 with anchored language. Output is a shaped verdict with named drivers — not a composite score.
Approach

Opinionated by design.

Most evaluation frameworks fail in predictable ways. They treat security as a scored factor rather than a gate. They accept ROI projections at face value rather than scoring methodology maturity. They ignore organizational readiness as if it were a deployment concern rather than an evaluation factor.

This instrument addresses each directly — anchored in practitioner experience evaluating tools in regulated environments, with structural weighting that prevents common evaluation failures.

Tier 0
Gates
4 · Binary

Four binary checks. One final fail stops the evaluation. Accumulated strength elsewhere cannot override a gate failure. The conditional state names blockers that, if resolved, allow the gate to resolve to pass — a resolution path, not a softening.

G1
Security & Data Handling
Industry-floor posture plus organizational fit. Specialists verify; the instrument carries weight.
G2
League / Industry Compliance
CBA-derived player data constraints, league AI policy, applicable privacy and biometric law.
G3
Org-Specific Legal Constraints
Two-layer: practitioner-surfaced deal-breakers plus specialist-deferred legal terms.
G4
Vendor Viability
POC and presentation as primary; current customers, funding, turnover as supporting.
Tier 1
Value
4 · Scored 1–5

The "should we adopt this at all" core. ROI is scored on defensibility under scrutiny, not the headline number. Methodology maturity (payback period, NPV awareness, sensitivity analysis, risk-adjustment) is required.

V1
Quantified ROI
Defensibility under scrutiny over headline numbers. Methodology maturity required.
V2
Magnitude of Problem Solved
Multi-stakeholder pain with active prior attempts is the marker of severity.
V3
Uniqueness & Substitutes
The Build-Onboard-Maintain test with switching-cost and time-horizon adjustments.
V4
Strategic Alignment
Two layers: strategic fit and organizational readiness. The right tool at the wrong moment is still the wrong adoption.
Tier 2
Cost of Ownership
5 · Scored 1–5

The true cost across the tool's lifetime, not headline price. For AI tools specifically, hybrid pricing (~41% of 2026 vendors) shifts unpredictable cost to the buyer once committed. The instrument scores against fully-loaded 3-year TCO at production scale.

C1
Direct Cost
Three-year TCO at production scale, including hidden categories and AI-specific usage exposure.
C2
Time-to-Value
Dual-source failure — internal capacity and vendor support. Scope creep, knowledge transfer.
C3
Integration Burden
Data integration leads. Plus ecosystem fit, identity, workflow positioning, durability over time.
C4
Ongoing Maintenance
The Three-Question Ownership Test: incentive, benefit, use. Plus succession.
C5
Switching Cost & Lock-in
Lock-in is leverage. AI-specific portability, model lock-in, integration depth.
Tier 3
Adoption
4 · Scored 1–5

The cluster that kills more tools than any other. Most adoption failures are not failures of the tool — they are failures of the organization's preparedness for the tool. Trust calibration matters more than headline accuracy for AI tools specifically.

A1
Adoption Magnetism
The pull a tool generates beyond functionality. Responsiveness is a prerequisite.
A2
Workflow Fit
Behavior change delta weighted by user-population change-tolerance and change-management.
A3
Champion Clarity
Distinct from operational ownership. Different roles, different skills — conflate at your peril.
A4
Trust & Explainability
Reliability plus context-appropriate confidence. The asymmetry of trust loss is structural.
Tier 4
Optional
4 · Context-selectable

Toggled per use case via reweighting profiles. Internal efficiency tools emphasize Tier 2 and 3, strategic capability tools emphasize Tier 1 and Innovation Velocity Cost, customer-facing tools emphasize reputational exposure and trust.

O1
Scalability Headroom
Three-year horizon weighted toward 18–24 months. AI landscape changes too fast otherwise.
O2
Vendor Partnership
Support quality, roadmap credibility, customer accessibility. Past delivery is the predictor.
O3
Reputational Exposure
Distinct from G1. Asymmetric damage from breach involving player, fan, or competitive data.
O4
Innovation Velocity Cost
The cost of not adopting. The only factor that asks what happens if the org stays put.
